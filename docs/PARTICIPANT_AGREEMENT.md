# 2026 MantisGrid AI Hackathon — Official Rules & Policies

## 1\. The Event

The 2026 MantisGrid AI Hackathon ("the Hackathon") is organized by MantisGrid
AI Inc ("the Organizer") and runs from September 17, 2026 from 9:30am to 3:00pm
(PDT), in person in Palo Alto, California. By registering, you agree to these
Rules and the Code of Conduct (Section 10). If anything here is unclear, or
you're unsure whether something is allowed, ask the organizers by emailing
hankunli@mantisgrid.ai — asking first is never penalized.

## 2\. Eligibility

Participants must be 18 or older to be eligible for prizes. Organizers, judges,
mentors, employees of MantisGrid AI and event sponsors, and their immediate
families may not compete, but are welcome as mentors, volunteers, and judges. 

## 3\. Teams

* Teams of up to 5 people. Each person may join only one team.  
* Team rosters may be changed (add/remove/swap members) until 24 hours before
  the event. After that, rosters are locked.  
* Every listed team member must have materially contributed to the project.

## 4\. What Counts as Your Project (Fresh Work Rule)

* All judged work must be created during the Hackathon. You may arrive with an
  idea, a plan, or even a project you've worked on before — but you may not
  reuse pre-existing code you wrote, and if you build on an existing project,
  judges will only consider functionality added during the event.  
* Open-source libraries, frameworks, starter kits, and the provided starter
  notebooks and templates are all fair game, provided you comply with their
  licenses and meaningfully build on them. A thin wrapper or reskin of an
  existing tool (including vllm-semantic-router or the OTel demo used as-is) will
  score poorly on Innovation and may be disqualified from awards.  
* Submit one project to one track. You may combine ideas across tracks; if you
  do, tell us which track's judging focus you want applied.  
* When time is up, stop building. Bug fixes and deployment repairs after the
  deadline are fine; new features are not.

## 5\. AI Usage & Disclosure

Participants may use any AI model, large language model (LLM), AI coding
assistant, agent framework, or open-source AI tool for development during the
hackathon. This is an AI infrastructure event — using AI heavily is expected,
not penalized.

In exchange, we require transparency:

* Your repository README must list the AI models, coding assistants, and agent
  frameworks you used, and briefly state what was AI-generated versus built by
  the team.  
* Judges may ask any team member to explain any part of the submission.
  Inability to explain your own architecture and code will hurt your Technical
  Execution score and may trigger review.  
* Misrepresenting AI-generated work as hand-built, or another team's/person's
  work as your own, is grounds for disqualification.

## 6\. Provided Resources, Data & API Keys

* Track resources (public infrastructure datasets including the starter
  notebooks, routing templates, sample workloads, OTel sample data) are
  provided under their respective licenses and terms; you are responsible for
  complying with them.  
* Any API keys issued for the event are per-team, must not be shared or
  committed to your repository, and will be revoked after the event. Usage may
  be rate-limited; abusive usage (e.g., using event keys for non-hackathon
  purposes) is grounds for disqualification.  
* Do not use datasets containing personal data you don't have rights to,
  proprietary data from an employer, or data obtained by scraping in violation
  of a site's terms.  

## 7\. Submissions

Submit at https://forms.gle/UbPSwZhKNfkovM8s5 before September 17, 2026 3:00pm
PDT. Late submissions are not judged. A complete submission includes:

1. Team information (all members, with student/career status).  
2. Project title and description, including your chosen track.  
3. A public source code repository — with API keys and secrets removed
   — including the AI-disclosure README from Section 5 and instructions to run
   the project.  
4. A presentation of around 4 minutes showing the project actually working.
   Including a demo is strongly encouraged.

Submissions must be in English and must not contain content that violates the Code of Conduct.

## 8\. Judging & Awards

Projects are scored by a judging panel against the published criteria:
Technical Execution (40%), Innovation / Wow Factor (30%), Potential Impact
(20%), Presentation / Demo (10%). Track-specific judging focus (data
storytelling; routing logic and explainability; usefulness on a real standard)
is applied within these criteria.

Track 1: model accuracy, explanability, strength of evaluations, token usage.
Track 2: use of MantisGrid AI/MCP, explanability of insights based on available
evidence, token usage.

Awards: Best Overall Project, Best of Track, Most Innovative of Track, Each
team is eligible to win at most one award.

## 10\. Code of Conduct

All attendees — hackers, mentors, judges, sponsors, volunteers, and organizers
— must follow the [MLH Code of
Conduct](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/code-of-conduct.md), at
the venue and in all event channels. It also applies to project content. Report
incidents to Hankun Li hankunli@mantisgrid.ai; reports may be made anonymously.
Have FUN — that's a rule too, but the Code of Conduct is how we make sure
everyone gets to.

## 11\. Disqualification

Organizers may, at their discretion, disqualify a team for violations including
but not limited to: breaking these Rules, breaking the Code of Conduct,
plagiarism or misrepresentation of work, misuse of provided API keys or data,
or other unsporting behavior. Where practical, teams will be given a chance to
respond before disqualification is final.
